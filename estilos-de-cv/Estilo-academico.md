**Estilo Académico / Research CV**

*El CV de la academia: publications dominantes, grants, conferences, peer review, tenure track institutional documentation*

# **Industrias donde funciona mejor**

Academic CV está concentrado en research universities, government research labs, research-intensive industries (pharma research, biotech research, AI research), think tanks, museums research.

### **Industrias de alta compatibilidad**

* **R1 research universities**

* **Postdoctoral positions**

* **Government research labs**

* **Research-intensive medical centers**

* **Major think tanks academic**

* **Industrial research labs**

### **Compatibilidad media**

* **Liberal arts colleges (SLACs)**

* **Research-intensive policy institutions**

* **Foundations with research arms**

# **Estructura exacta del CV**

Academic CV structure es highly conventional y discipline-specific. Order varies by field (humanities differ from STEM; STEM further differ in life sciences vs physical sciences vs computer science). General sections universally expected: Contact, Education, Academic Positions, Publications, Grants, Teaching, Service, Awards. Order y prominence varies.

### **Orden canónico de secciones**

1. 1\. Contact information: name, institutional affiliation, professional address, professional phone, professional email, ORCID iD, personal website / academic homepage, optional Google Scholar profile link

2. 2\. Education: highest degree first, with full thesis/dissertation title, advisor name, year, institution. PhD particularly important.

3. 3\. Academic Positions: cronología inversa, with academic title, institution, dates. Includes postdocs, visiting positions, sabbaticals.

4. 4\. Honors & Awards: prestigious fellowships, prizes, named awards, distinctions. Society memberships, named lectureships.

5. 5\. Publications: typically the dominant section. Substructured by type:   \- Peer-reviewed journal articles   \- Books / monographs (humanities y social sciences emphasize)   \- Book chapters   \- Edited volumes (as editor)   \- Conference proceedings (CS, engineering)   \- Pre-prints (when relevant; STEM increasingly)   \- Working papers (economics, political science)   \- Translations (humanities, when relevant)

6. 6\. Grants & Funding: cronología inversa, with grant agency, grant title, role (PI, co-PI, co-I, senior personnel), amount, dates, brief description.

7. 7\. Invited Talks / Conference Presentations: substructured by:   \- Keynote / plenary lectures   \- Invited talks (named lectures, distinguished lectures)   \- Conference presentations   \- Symposia organized   \- Workshops led

8. 8\. Teaching: courses taught (with institution, year, enrollment if relevant), teaching evaluations summary, teaching awards, course development.

9. 9\. Mentoring & Advising:   \- PhD students supervised (current y completed, with thesis titles, current positions of completed)   \- Postdocs supervised   \- Master's students   \- Undergraduate research students

10. 10\. Editorial Service:    \- Editor-in-Chief / Co-Editor positions    \- Associate Editor positions    \- Editorial Board memberships    \- Reviewer for journals (list)    \- Reviewer for grant agencies (list)

11. 11\. Professional Service:    \- Society memberships y offices held    \- Conference organizing    \- Tenure y promotion external review    \- Government advisory committees    \- Institutional service (committees at home institution)

12. 12\. Outreach / Public Engagement:    \- Media appearances / journalism    \- Op-eds y popular writing    \- Public lectures    \- Science communication    \- Policy engagement

13. 13\. Languages: relevant para roles requiring multilingualism (humanities, area studies)

14. 14\. Memberships in Professional Societies

15. 15\. References: in academic CV, references are explicitly listed with names, titles, institutions, contact information. Different from industry resume convention.

### **Jerarquía visual**

Hierarchy via section ordering y density. Publications es invariably most prominent. Grants strongly emphasized in STEM. Teaching emphasized in liberal arts colleges. Mentoring emphasized for senior researchers. Editorial service emphasized for established researchers.

### **Longitud y densidad**

Academic CV embraces extensive length. Brevity is NOT a virtue here.

Postdoc: 4-8 pages

Assistant Professor: 6-12 pages

Associate Professor (tenure): 10-20 pages

Full Professor: 20-50+ pages

Distinguished/Named Professor: 30-100+ pages

Senior eminent researchers: 100-200+ pages routine

# **Secciones obligatorias, opcionales y prohibidas**

### **Obligatorias**

* **Education with thesis/dissertation details:** Each degree with full thesis title, advisor name, committee members optionally, year, institution. PhD particularly important: thesis is intellectual identity.

* **Academic Positions cronología completa:** Every academic position held: postdocs, visiting positions, lectureships, instructorships, professorial roles. With dates exact.

* **Publications comprehensive:** EVERY peer-reviewed publication. No selective filtering. Journal name, volume, pages, year. Co-authors in correct order. DOI when available.

* **Grants & Funding:** Every grant received con role, amount, agency, dates. STEM emphasizes; humanities less but still important.

* **Teaching:** Every course taught con year, institution, level (undergrad/grad), course number, enrollment if substantial.

* **Service:** Editorial roles, peer review, conference organizing, committee service, society memberships y offices.

### **Opcionales (según contexto)**

* **Honors & Awards:** Prestigious awards, fellowships, prizes.

* **Mentoring:** Students supervised. Cada vez más important para senior researchers.

* **Outreach / Public engagement:** Particularly important for impact-driven scholarship, policy-relevant work.

* **Languages:** For humanities, area studies, comparative work.

* **Patents:** STEM, especially engineering, materials science, biotech.

* **Software / Datasets / Open code:** For computer science, computational social science, open science contributors.

* **Citation metrics:** Some senior researchers include h-index, total citations, i10-index from Google Scholar.

* **References:** Academic CV explicitly lists references unlike industry resume.

### **Prohibidas o desaconsejadas**

* **Foto, DOB, marital status (USA market):** Inappropriate. Compliance reasons.

* **Personal interests genéricos:** 'Hiking, reading, cooking' wastes space.

* **Skill bars o competency ratings:** Inappropriate.

* **Visual decoration:** Color blocks, iconos, illustration, custom typography expressivos. Inappropriate.

* **Selective filtering of publications:** Showing only 'best' publications signals lack of confidence in full record. List all peer-reviewed publications.

* **Inflated co-authorship roles:** Listing yourself as 'lead author' when you were second author. Career-ending if discovered.

# **Características visuales y diseño**

Academic CV es deliberately conventional y institutional. Sin design awareness por choice. The convention is the message: this is a professional document for evaluation against standardized rubrics.

### **Layout y columnas**

Single column estricto

Section headers consistent: bold, slightly larger, sometimes uppercase

Subsection headers when needed

Bullets simple or numbered lists for publications

Hanging indents for publications (first line flush left, subsequent lines indented)

### **Espaciado, márgenes y whitespace**

Interlineado 1.0-1.15

Espaciado entre sections 8-12pt

Espaciado entre items 4-6pt

Márgenes 1 inch (US Letter) or 2.5cm (A4)

### **Iconografía y elementos gráficos**

Sin iconos. Anti-academic.

Discrete academic identifiers acceptable: ORCID iD logo, Google Scholar logo (small)

### **Densidad de información y balance visual**

Densidad alta. Information density is feature.

Each line carries verifiable information.

Whitespace minimal por section delimiters only.

# **Sistema tipográfico**

Typography Academic CV es conventional serif tradicional. Times New Roman es default safe. Garamond, Palatino, Cambria son acceptable alternatives. Sans-serif less common pero acceptable in some fields (computer science, increasingly).

### **Tipografías recomendadas**

* **Times New Roman:** Universal default. ATS-safe. Body 11-12pt, headers 12-14pt, name 14-18pt. Conservative y expected.

* **Garamond / Adobe Garamond Pro:** Slightly more elegant. Body 11-12pt. Acceptable in humanities.

* **Palatino:** Classic humanist serif. Body 11pt. Common in linguistics, philosophy, literature.

* **Cambria:** Microsoft default serif. Modern y readable. Body 11pt. Cross-disciplinary.

* **Computer Modern (LaTeX default):** Default in mathematics, physics, theoretical computer science. Comunica LaTeX fluency y disciplinary identity.

* **Calibri / Arial:** Sans-serif acceptable in computer science, increasingly. Body 10-11pt.

### **Jerarquía de tamaños**

Name: 14-18pt

Section headers: 12-14pt bold often uppercase

Subsection headers: 11-12pt bold

Body: 11-12pt

References / footnotes / supplementary: 10-11pt

### **Tipografías a evitar**

Custom display typefaces decorative

Comic Sans, decoratives: descalificantes

Multiple font families mixed sin coherence

Light weights (\<400) en body

# **Uso del color**

Color en Academic CV es minimal o ausente. Black on white standard. Color is unprofessional in academic context.

### **Paletas recomendadas**

* **Pure black/white:** Standard. Black text on white. Cero color.

* **Black \+ dark accent:** Black text \+ name in dark navy or dark gray. Subtle differentiation acceptable.

# **Estilo de redacción**

Writing Academic CV es deliberately neutral, factual, conventional. Each entry is verifiable claim. No marketing language, no superlatives, no autopromocional phrasing.

### **Tono y voz**

Tono neutral, factual, informational. Communicates competence sin self-promotion.

Voz primera persona ausente; entries are factual statements.

### **Verbos de acción y estructura de frases**

Generally verbs are minimized; entries are factual statements:

  \- 'Awarded NSF Graduate Fellowship, 2014-2017'

  \- 'Co-authored monograph published with Cambridge University Press, 2023'

  \- 'Served as Associate Editor, Journal of \[Field\], 2020-present'

When verbs used: Published, Co-authored, Edited, Translated, Presented, Delivered, Awarded, Funded, Supervised, Taught, Co-taught, Served, Organized

### **Uso de métricas, KPIs y storytelling**

Citation metrics sometimes included for senior researchers: 'h-index \= 47, total citations \= 12,348 (Google Scholar, 2026)'

Grant amounts in dollars: '$1,847,000 NSF CAREER award (PI), 2022-2027'

Publication counts: 'Peer-reviewed journal articles: 87 (47 first or last author)'

Teaching evaluations: 'Teaching evaluations: mean 4.7/5.0 across 14 courses, 2018-2025'

Mentees: 'Supervised 14 PhD students (8 completed, 6 current); 6 alumni in tenure-track positions'

# **Compatibilidad ATS**

Academic CV is parsed by academic-specific systems (Interfolio, Slate by Technolutions, faculty applicant tracking) more than typical industry ATS. Parsing for academic CVs es structurally different: handles long documents, expects publications lists, parses grants y other sections.

### **Score ATS estimado**

### **Elementos que mejoran el parsing**

* Conventional headers: 'Education', 'Publications', 'Grants', 'Teaching', 'Service'

* PDF generation from LaTeX or Word, not scanned

* Embed any custom fonts properly

* Maintain hanging indents for publications consistently

* Date formats consistent

* ORCID iD prominently

* DOIs included when possible (clickable)

### **Elementos que rompen ATS**

* Multi-column layouts (uncommon in Academic CV anyway)

* Tables for layout decorative

* Iconography decorative

* Custom fonts not embedded

* Excessive PDF compression flattening text
